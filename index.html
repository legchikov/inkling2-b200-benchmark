Throughput scaling

Without speculation one stream yields 201 tok/s at a 4.83 ms TPOT. Concurrency 192 fills the batch and reaches 5,460 tok/s (1,365 per GPU) — a 27.2× increase in aggregate throughput. Without speculation, per-user speed falls from 207 to 35 tok/s; the dashed line shows per-user speed with DSpark on the right axis.

Speculative decoding, measured — not simulated

DSpark drafts up to 8 tokens per step with the bundled MTP head. The launch numbers assume a fixed 3.5 accepted tokens per step; we measured acceptance per request instead, and it depends on what you generate: 7.1 of 8 on synthetic random text, 4.6–6.5 of 8 on SPEED-Bench prompts. On a real prompt the win is smaller but still large: 4.4 tokens accepted per step gives 535 tok/s for one stream (TPOT 1.87 ms).

Latency under load

For the non-speculative run, mean time to first token is under 300 ms through concurrency 16 and increases to about five seconds at concurrency 192. These measurements include both waiting and processing.

Long-context performance

At concurrency 1, output throughput falls by approximately 1.8× between 16K and 128K input tokens. Mean time to first token at 128K is 1.16 s. These tests do not measure the model's full 1M-token context.

Prefill cost

MegaMoE — fused DeepGEMM dispatch and combine — keeps expert-heavy prefill fast: 16K tokens on a single request take 168 ms, roughly 98K tokens per second of prompt processing. Batch 32 of the same size costs 2744 ms.

Summary and limitations

Key results

  • 6,634 tok/s aggregate — 1,659 per GPU at concurrency 64 on synthetic inputs.
  • 785 tok/s measured for a single synthetic stream, which is 891 tok/s per user once the first token is out (TPOT 1.12 ms, TTFT 153 ms).
  • 3.86M tokens in the reported KV pool without speculation. This is not a guarantee of concurrent conversation capacity.

Limitations

  • Four GPUs only. TP8 performance and cache capacity were not measured in this study.
  • Workload matters. Acceptance was higher on this synthetic workload than on the real-prompt datasets; results may differ for other tasks.
  • DSpark reduces the reported KV pool from 3.86M to 2.57M tokens in these configurations. The trade-off depends on the workload.
  • Decode-heavy mix only: no prefix-cache reuse, no disaggregated prefill, no HiCache.
  • Run-to-run variance is about 5–10%; a second full sweep thirty minutes later differed by that much at peak concurrency.
